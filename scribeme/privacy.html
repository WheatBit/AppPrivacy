BookWrite: ScribeMe — Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 18, 2026

The short version: ScribeMe keeps your writing on your device. It doesn't collect your data, doesn't track you, and doesn't send anything to us. There are no accounts and no servers of ours. Dictation turns your voice into words on your device. If iCloud sync is on, your books sync through your own private iCloud — still never to us. From version 1.7, ScribeMe also keeps one Safety Copy of your library in your own iCloud Drive, or on your device if iCloud Drive is off.

Who this covers

This policy applies to the ScribeMe writing app for iPhone and iPad (listed on the App Store as BookWrite: ScribeMe, and formerly named Penna) and for Android (on Google Play as BookWrite: ScribeMe), published by Methodical Enterprises LLC ("we," "us"). The sections below describe the iPhone and iPad app; On Android says how the same promises work there.

What we collect

Nothing. ScribeMe has no user accounts and no backend servers. We never receive your books, drafts, notes, drawings, or any information about how you use the app. If you email us, for example with Send Feedback in ScribeMe's Settings, we receive only what you choose to send, and use it only to answer you. The same goes for Send a Theme Idea and Ask the Maker: each opens an email to us in your own mail app, and ScribeMe itself sends nothing.

What stays on your device

Everything you write in ScribeMe — your books, chapters, notes, drawings, illustrations, and settings — is stored on your device. This information never leaves your device except through your own iCloud device backup, the iCloud sync described below, the Safety Copy (from version 1.7), which keeps one copy of your library in your own iCloud Drive when iCloud Drive is on, or when you deliberately export or share your work yourself. None of these reach us.

iCloud sync (optional)

ScribeMe can keep your shelf in sync across your own devices. A new library starts with sync off. You turn it on in ScribeMe's Settings, and the change takes effect the next time the app starts. When sync is on:

  • Your library — books, pages, drawings, pictures, story notes, and writing history — syncs through your own private iCloud database, under your Apple Account.
  • We have no access to it. We operate no servers and cannot read, retrieve, or delete anything you sync. Apple's handling of iCloud data is governed by Apple's own privacy policy.
  • Turn it off at any time, and from the next start ScribeMe keeps your work only on the device. Copies already in your iCloud stay there until you delete them.

If you update from an earlier version and your library was already syncing to iCloud, ScribeMe turns the switch on for you, so your books keep syncing. It does this only if you have never changed the switch yourself. You can turn it off in Settings at any time.

In versions up to and including 1.3, the library could sync to your own private iCloud even with the switch off. Version 1.4 fixes this: with the switch off, your library stays on the device. Either way, it never reached us.

Safety Copy

From version 1.7, ScribeMe keeps one Safety Copy of your whole library, so a lost or new phone, or deleting the app, doesn't lose your books. It needs no account and works whether iCloud sync is on or off. ScribeMe makes it by itself when you leave the app after your library has changed, at most once an hour, and replaces the last copy each time. It never replaces a copy with an empty library.

  • Where it is. When iCloud Drive is on, the copy is kept in iCloud Drive → ScribeMe, in your own iCloud Drive under your Apple Account. You can see it in the Files app, and on your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account. When iCloud Drive is off, it is kept on your device, in On My iPhone (or On My iPad) → ScribeMe in the Files app. There is only ever one copy.
  • What's in it. The same as Back Up All Books: every book (including those in the Trash), its pages, drawings, pictures, cover art, Mood Board photos, and story notes, plus your shelves, the days you wrote, and your ScribeMe settings. Photos of your own shelf pieces are left out and stay on the device. Locked books and diaries are included, and are locked again when they come back into ScribeMe, but the file itself has no lock or password: it is protected like your other files, by your device passcode and your Apple Account.
  • We have no access to it. ScribeMe writes the file only to your iCloud Drive or your device and never sends it to us. Apple's handling of iCloud Drive is governed by Apple's own privacy policy.
  • Bringing books back. If ScribeMe opens to an empty library with iCloud sync off and finds a Safety Copy, it asks before bringing the books back. Settings → Your Library → Safety Copy shows when the last copy was made and where it is.
  • Removing it. There is no switch for it in ScribeMe. You can delete the file in the Files app at any time; ScribeMe makes a new one the next time your library changes. To keep it out of iCloud Drive, turn off iCloud Drive for ScribeMe in your device's iCloud settings.

Face ID and passcode locks

You can lock any book so it opens only after Face ID, Touch ID, or your device passcode. This check is performed entirely by iOS. ScribeMe is told only whether the check succeeded — no biometric information is ever available to the app, to us, or to anyone else. If your device has no passcode set, there is nothing to check against, and a locked book opens normally. A locked book's title still shows on your shelf.

Dictation

You can speak your story instead of typing it. The first time, iOS asks permission for ScribeMe to use the microphone and speech recognition. Speech is turned into words on your device: ScribeMe requires on-device recognition, and on a device that can't do it, dictation is simply unavailable rather than sending your voice to Apple's servers. Your voice is not recorded or kept; only the words go onto the page. Nothing is sent to us. You can turn off either permission at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security.

Photos

To add an illustration to a page, a photo to a cover, or a photo to a book's Mood Board, ScribeMe uses Apple's photo picker. You choose a photo, and only that photo is given to ScribeMe. The app never sees the rest of your photo library, so it does not ask for photo library permission. Photos you pick are made smaller and saved with your book. Mood Board photos are also checked for unsuitable content on your device, with the same built-in model as the shelf camera. They are never sent to us.

Cover art, read-aloud, and audiobooks

Cover illustrations can use Apple's Image Playground, and read-aloud and audiobook export use Apple's speech synthesis. Both run on your device through Apple's own frameworks and are subject to Apple's privacy policy. Neither sends your writing to us. You can turn off AI illustrations in ScribeMe's Settings.

Your own shelf pieces (camera)

You can photograph something of your own, such as a keepsake or a figure, and stand it on your shelf. The first time, iOS asks permission for ScribeMe to use the camera; you can also pick a photo instead. The background is removed, and the picture is checked for unsuitable content, entirely on your device using Apple's frameworks and a model built into the app. The picture is saved with your shelf (and syncs only if iCloud sync is on). It is never sent to us. You can turn off camera permission at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security.

Widgets, controls, Siri, and iPhone search

ScribeMe's Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets can show your words today, your streak, a story idea, and the titles and cover colours of the books you choose. The app hands these to its widget on your device; your page text is never included. During a writing sprint, a Live Activity shows the book's title on your Lock Screen. Control Center and Action button controls only open ScribeMe.

ScribeMe tells iOS the titles and author names of your books so that Siri can understand "add to my book" and so that unlocked books appear in iPhone search. Locked books are left out of search. This index is kept by iOS on your device; page text is never included.

When you use ScribeMe with Siri (for example "Add to my book", "Write in my diary", "Give me a story idea", or "Start a writing sprint"), Siri handles what you say under Apple's privacy policy, and ScribeMe saves the words in your library on your device. Siri can add words to a book but ScribeMe never reads your pages back to Siri. Adding to a locked book or a diary requires your iPhone to be unlocked. If you use Apple Intelligence to create a journal entry, ScribeMe saves it in a book called My Diary; it can only be written, never read back.

Exporting and sharing

When you export a PDF, booklet, EPUB, text file, audiobook, book file, or backup file, that file is created on your device and goes wherever you send it using the iOS share sheet. We are not involved and receive no copy.

Notifications

If you turn on the daily writing prompt, iOS asks permission to show notifications, and ScribeMe schedules them locally on your device. There is no push server of ours, and no message of any kind is sent to us.

Optional purchases

ScribeMe is free, and every writing tool in it stays free. You can choose to leave a tip or become a Supporter. These purchases are made and handled entirely by Apple through the App Store, under Apple's privacy policy. We never see your name, your Apple Account, or your payment details; Apple gives us only sales totals, never who bought what. ScribeMe asks Apple, on your device, only which purchases this Apple Account owns, so it can unlock Supporter themes and restore them; it keeps the result on your device and sends nothing to us. On Android says how Google Play handles the same purchases.

Analytics, tracking, and advertising

ScribeMe contains no analytics, no third-party SDKs, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind. We do not build a profile of you and do not track you across apps or websites. There are no subscriptions and no ads.

Children's privacy

ScribeMe does not collect personal information from anyone, including children. It is safe for writers of all ages.

Keeping and deleting your data

Because your writing lives on your device (and, if sync is on or the Safety Copy is in iCloud Drive, your own iCloud), you are always in control. Deleting the app removes its data from the device, including a Safety Copy kept there. A Safety Copy in iCloud Drive is meant to outlast the app: it stays until you delete it in the Files app (iCloud Drive → ScribeMe). To remove synced copies, delete them from your iCloud storage. We hold nothing to delete, because we never had it. Please keep your own backups of writing you want to preserve — ScribeMe can save your whole library to a single file, and from version 1.7 it also keeps the Safety Copy for you.

On Android

The Android app keeps the same promises: no account, no servers of ours, no analytics of ours, no advertising, and nothing about you or your writing is ever sent to us. Where Android works differently from iOS:

  • Storage and backup. Your books are stored on your phone. Android's own backup can keep an encrypted copy in your Google account, under Google's privacy policy; you control it in Android's settings. There is no iCloud sync on Android.
  • Safety Copy. The Android app keeps one Safety Copy of your library too, made by itself when your writing changes (at most once an hour). It holds the same things as on iPhone; locked books are included, and the file has no lock of its own. Until you choose where to keep it, it stays in the app's own storage on your phone, which Android's backup leaves out and which is removed with the app. ScribeMe asks once, after your first book, and Settings lets you choose a file with Android's file picker, for example in your own cloud drive or Downloads. A copy in a cloud drive is handled under that service's privacy policy. There is no switch to turn it off; you can delete the file you chose at any time. It never comes to us.
  • Book locks use Android's fingerprint, face or screen lock. Android does the check; ScribeMe is told only whether it succeeded.
  • Dictation. The first time you tap the microphone, Android asks permission for ScribeMe to use it. Speech is turned into words by your phone's own on-device recogniser; on a phone that can't do that, dictation is unavailable rather than sending your voice anywhere. If your phone needs its speech model for your language, ScribeMe asks Android to fetch it, and Android does that download itself. Your voice is not recorded or kept. You can turn the permission off at any time in Android's settings.
  • Photos. Covers, illustrations and the Mood Board use Android's photo picker, so ScribeMe sees only the photos you choose and asks for no photo permission.
  • Your own shelf pieces (camera). In Decorate, you can photograph something of your own for the shelf, or choose a photo instead. The photo is taken by your phone's own camera app, so ScribeMe asks for no camera permission. ScribeMe checks the photo on your phone, then cuts the object out of its background on your phone with Google's ML Kit. Only the cut-out is kept, in your library; the photo itself is deleted, and it is never sent to us or to Google.
  • Google's ML Kit diagnostics. ML Kit runs inside Google Play services on your phone. Google says it sends Google diagnostic information about how it runs: your device's make, model and Android version, the app's name and version, performance and error details, and an identifier for this installation. It doesn't include your photos or your writing. Google uses it to keep ML Kit working, under Google's privacy policy, and ScribeMe has no switch for it. None of it comes to us.
  • Read-aloud and audiobooks use your phone's own text-to-speech voice. Cover art is drawn or picked by you; there are no AI illustrations on Android.
  • Widgets, shortcuts and tiles. Home screen widgets, app shortcuts and Quick Settings tiles can show your words today, a story idea and the titles of the books you choose, but never page text and never a locked book's title. A writing sprint's notification never names a locked book. There is no Siri or Google Assistant integration.
  • Notifications for sprints and the daily prompt are scheduled on your phone. Android asks your permission first.
  • Tips and Supporters. You can leave a tip or become a Supporter. Payment goes through Google Play, under Google's privacy policy, using Google's Play Billing library, which talks to Google Play on your phone. ScribeMe never sees your payment details; it keeps only which purchases your Google account owns, on your phone, and sends nothing to us.
  • Exporting and sharing use Android's share sheet and file picker. We receive no copy.
