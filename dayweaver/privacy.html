DayWeaver — Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 12, 2026

The short version: DayWeaver keeps your day on your device. It doesn't collect your data, doesn't track you, and never sends anything to us — we run no servers and have no accounts. It reads your Calendar and Reminders, and saves changes to them only when you ask it to. The current version makes no network connections at all.

Who this covers

This policy applies to the DayWeaver app for iPhone (formerly named Weave), including its Share extension and home screen widget, published by Methodical Enterprises LLC ("we," "us"). By using DayWeaver you agree to this policy.

What we collect

Nothing. DayWeaver has no accounts of ours and no backend servers. We never receive your tasks, notes, calendar events, reminders, habits, or any other information. We cannot see how you use the app.

What stays on your device

The things you capture in DayWeaver, your habits, your preferences, and your settings are stored only on your iPhone, in the app's own storage. A copy of the day's plan is kept in storage shared with the DayWeaver widget so it can show it. None of this leaves your device except through features of iOS that you control, such as your own iCloud device backup, or when you save entries to Calendar or Reminders as described below.

Calendar and Reminders

DayWeaver asks for full access to your Calendar and Reminders. It reads your events and reminders so it can show them together with your tasks on one timeline. It also writes to them, but only when you do something that asks for it:

  • Sync button. Things you capture in DayWeaver stay in the app until you tap the sync button ("sync … to calendar" or "to reminders"). Then DayWeaver saves them as new events in your default calendar or new reminders in your default list, with their title, notes, time, and repeat. The same button moves entries DayWeaver created to the times it has since arranged for them.
  • Your edits. When you edit, drag to a new time, tick off, or delete an event or reminder in DayWeaver, that change is saved to Calendar or Reminders.
  • Nothing is written on its own. Arranging your day, carrying unfinished captures forward to today, and reading a screenshot happen only inside the app. Events and reminders DayWeaver didn't create are never moved by it; they change only when you edit them yourself.

Anything DayWeaver saves to Calendar or Reminders is handled like any change you make there yourself: it syncs to wherever that calendar or list lives, such as your iCloud account or another account you added in iOS Settings. It is never sent to us. You can revoke access at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars / Reminders.

Google Calendar and Google Tasks

The current version of DayWeaver does not sign in to Google and makes no network connections. Google calendars you have added to your iPhone in iOS Settings appear in DayWeaver through Apple Calendar, as described above.

DayWeaver contains an optional direct Google connection that is turned off in the current version. If it is turned on, it works like this:

  • It does nothing until you choose to sign in. You sign in on Google's own sign-in page, shown by iOS. DayWeaver never sees or handles your Google password.
  • DayWeaver asks for read-only access to your calendar and tasks. It reads the events on your main Google calendar and the tasks in your Google Tasks lists, only to show them on your device. It never changes anything in your Google account.
  • Your device talks to Google directly. Nothing passes through us — we operate no servers and never receive your Google data.
  • The sign-in tokens are stored in your device's Keychain, not in the app's ordinary storage.
  • Turning a Google source off in DayWeaver stops DayWeaver from reading it. To remove DayWeaver's access completely, revoke it in your Google Account's security settings. Google's handling of your data is governed by Google's own privacy policy.

Apple Intelligence

On iPhones with iOS 26 and Apple Intelligence turned on, DayWeaver uses Apple's on-device language model to tidy the title of something you capture and estimate how long it takes, and to suggest a change when an evening is too full. This runs entirely on your device. Without Apple Intelligence, DayWeaver reads your words with its own parser, also on your device. Either way, what you type is never sent to us.

Adding a schedule from screenshots

You can pick screenshots of a schedule with the iOS photo picker. Only the images you pick are given to DayWeaver; it does not ask for access to your photo library. Apple's Vision framework reads the text in them on your device. DayWeaver doesn't keep the images; it keeps only the items you confirm, as captures in the app.

The Share extension

DayWeaver includes a Share extension so you can send text or a web link into DayWeaver from other apps. What you share, and any note you type with it, is stored on your device and picked up by the app the next time you open it. It is not transmitted anywhere, and DayWeaver does not open the link.

Widget

The DayWeaver widget shows the day the app last arranged, including the titles of your events, reminders, and tasks. It reads them from storage on your device and shows them on your Home Screen, where anyone holding your phone can see them.

Alarms

If you ask for an alarm on an item, DayWeaver asks for permission to send notifications the first time, then schedules the alarm locally on your device. The notification shows the item's title and time. There is no push server and nothing is sent to us. You can turn notifications off in iOS Settings → Notifications.

Analytics, tracking, and advertising

DayWeaver contains no analytics, no third-party SDKs, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind. We do not build a profile of you and do not track you across apps or websites. There are no purchases, subscriptions, or ads.

Children's privacy

DayWeaver does not collect personal information from anyone, including children. It is safe for users of all ages.

Keeping and deleting your data

Because your data lives on your device, you are always in control. By default, DayWeaver forgets a captured item once its date is more than 7 days past; you can change this to 90 days or never in Settings, and clear all captured items there at any time. Deleting the DayWeaver app removes the data it keeps in its own storage. Events and reminders it saved to Calendar or Reminders belong to those apps and stay there until you delete them. We hold nothing to delete, because we never had it.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update the date at the top of this page and post the new version here.
