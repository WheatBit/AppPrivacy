Book Tracker: Lexi — Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 28, 2026

The short version: Lexi keeps your library on your device. There are no accounts, no ads and no tracking. If you choose to read with friends, what you share with them (your place in a shared book, your notes on it, messages and book clubs) travels through Apple's iCloud. Photos of your books' spines are shared only if you turn that on. Looking up a book sends its barcode number or title to public book databases.

Who this covers

This policy applies to the Lexi reading-tracker app for iPhone and iPad (listed on the App Store as Book Tracker: Lexi, and formerly named Lunara), published by Methodical Enterprises LLC ("we," "us").

What stays on your device

Your books, reading progress, reading log, notes, shelves, photos you take of covers and spines, and settings are stored on your device. Lexi has no user accounts and we run no servers. This information leaves your device only through features of iOS you control (such as your own iCloud device backup) and the features described below, each of which you choose to use.

Looking up a book online

When you scan a barcode, search for a book, or open an author's other books, Lexi sends that book's ISBN, or its title and author, to public book databases to fill in its details and cover:

  • Open Library (openlibrary.org), run by the Internet Archive.
  • Google Books (googleapis.com).
  • Apple Books search (itunes.apple.com).

Only the book's identifier or title is sent. Your notes, reading progress, library, and any information about you are never included, and no account or device identifier is attached. These services have their own privacy policies and see the network request, including your IP address, as with any web request. You can always add a book by typing it in yourself.

Reading together with friends

Reading together is optional. Friends are added only in person, by scanning each other's code. If you use it, Lexi stores the following in Apple's CloudKit (part of iCloud), in Lexi's public database, so your friends' devices can receive it:

  • A random reader ID, made on your device. It is what your friend code contains; it is not your name, Apple ID or phone number.
  • For books you choose to share with a friend: your place in the book and the notes you write on it, with where in the book each note opens.
  • Messages you send, with the time and the reader IDs of the sender and recipient, and book clubs you create or join: their name, members' reader IDs, and the books on their shelf.
  • If you report a message or note, a copy of what it said and its ID, so it can be reviewed.

The names you give your friends stay on your device and are never uploaded. To tell you when a message arrives, Lexi asks iCloud to notify your device of messages addressed to your reader ID; the notification's text is written on your device. Reading together needs an iCloud account. Apple stores this data under its own privacy policy, and we can see it only through Apple's CloudKit tools, which we use solely to review reports and remove content that breaks Lexi's community rules.

The spine library

Lexi can show your book the real spine of its edition, photographed by another Lexi reader. To find one, Lexi asks iCloud for spines by a code derived from the book's ISBN (or its title and author). You can turn this off in Settings → Spine Library.

If you turn on Share my spine photos, the photos you take of your own books' spines are stored in Lexi's public CloudKit database, with the book's ISBN or title and a random contributor ID (separate from your reader ID), so readers with the same edition can see them. Only spine photos are shared, never covers or other pictures, and only when the lettering on the photo names the book. Any shared spine can be reported and removed.

Camera and photo library

Lexi can use the camera to scan a book's barcode, to photograph a cover or spine, and to read a whole shelf of spines from one photo. Barcode frames and shelf photos are processed on your device and not kept. Cover and spine photos you take, or choose from your photo library, are stored on your device as that book's image; a spine photo leaves your device only if you turn on spine sharing, as described above. You can revoke access at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security.

Analytics, tracking, and advertising

Lexi contains no analytics, no advertising SDKs, and no tracking of any kind. We do not build a profile of you and do not track you across apps or websites.

Children's privacy

Lexi does not ask for anyone's name, email or age, and its reading-together features connect only people who have met in person to scan each other's code.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your library lives on your device, and deleting Lexi removes it. What you shared through reading together or the spine library stays in iCloud until it is removed. Blocking or removing a friend removes their content from your device. To have everything you shared deleted, email us the code shown in Settings → Reading Friends and we will delete it.
