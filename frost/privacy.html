Frost — Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 12, 2026

The short version: Frost is an encrypted group messenger for iPhone. There is no phone number, no email and no account. Your keys are made on your iPhone and never leave it. Unlike our other apps, Frost uses a server of ours, called the postbox. It passes sealed messages between phones and deletes them after seven days. It is built so that we can't read them, and it stores no names, no phone numbers and no IP addresses. Notifications never show a message, screenshots of Frost come out blank, and nothing can be shared out of the app. Frost is a test build and has not been independently audited, so please don't trust it with real secrets yet.

Who this covers

This policy applies to Frost, an end-to-end encrypted group messenger for iPhone, published by Methodical Enterprises LLC ("we," "us"). "Frost" is a working name, and the name may change.

A test build, not yet audited

Frost is a test prototype. Its encryption uses MLS, an open standard for group messaging, with post-quantum keys. Each piece is tested on its own, but the whole app has not yet been tested from end to end on two phones, and no independent security audit has been done. An audit comes before real use. Until then, don't use Frost for anything you need to keep secret. This page describes how Frost is built to work. A flaw found later could mean it doesn't fully protect what this page says it protects.

What stays on your iPhone

The name you choose, your keys, your chats, your contacts' names and keys, your notification disguise and your settings are stored on your iPhone. Frost never asks for your phone number or email address, and it doesn't read your contacts.

  • Your keys are kept in the iPhone's Keychain. They can be read only while the phone is unlocked, and they are never copied to backups or other devices.
  • Your chats are saved in an encrypted file that can't be read while the phone is locked. It is left out of iCloud and computer backups.
  • Each message waits sealed under its own key until you open it. Opening it destroys that key, so a message can be opened only once.
  • Frost needs Face ID or your passcode to open, and it locks again whenever you leave it. It won't run on an iPhone without a passcode.

Our server: the postbox

Phones in a chat can't reach each other directly, so Frost passes messages through a server we run at relay.methodical-ep.com, called the postbox. Each chat has its own mailbox there. Your phone leaves sealed messages in the mailbox and collects new ones from it. While Frost is open and unlocked, it checks your mailboxes every couple of seconds. All traffic goes over HTTPS.

What the postbox receives:

  • Mailbox ids. Random numbers made on a phone. They don't contain or point to anyone's name, phone or account.
  • An access token for each mailbox. Made on the phones from a secret the postbox never sees. The postbox keeps only a scrambled form (a SHA-256 hash) to check it.
  • Sealed messages. Everything a phone sends, including messages, read receipts, group changes and contact cards, is encrypted on the phone and padded to exactly the same size (8,192 bytes). The keys to open them exist only on the phones in that chat. The postbox can't tell what kind of message it holds or how long it is.
  • Sealed push handles, if notifications are set up. See Notifications below.

What the postbox stores, and for how long:

  • Sealed messages, each stamped only with the hour it arrived. Each one is deleted seven days after it arrives, whether or not anyone has read it. Deleted rows are overwritten, not just marked as free.
  • Mailbox records: the random id, the hashed access token, and a running count used to keep messages in order. These are not deleted when a chat ends, but they say nothing about who used them.
  • Sealed push handles, until Apple reports that the phone no longer has Frost. See below.

What the postbox doesn't store: names, phone numbers, email addresses, keys, message text, the exact time of any message, IP addresses, or Apple push tokens. It has no accounts. Its logs hold only totals, such as how many messages arrived in the last ten minutes, never which mailbox or who. We don't back up the postbox.

What it can still see while running: like any server, the postbox can't avoid seeing some things in the moment it answers a request. Your IP address reaches the server so it can answer you; the web server in front of the postbox keeps no logs and doesn't pass your address on to the postbox, and nothing records it. Someone in control of the running server could also watch the timing of requests and which mailboxes are checked together. And so that your phone isn't woken for your own message, it shows the postbox its sealed push handle when it sends; the postbox can't open the handle, but it can tell which of the phones in that mailbox sent the message. None of this is written down, but you should know it is possible.

Notifications

To tell you a message is waiting, Frost uses Apple's push notification service (APNs). Frost asks permission to show notifications when you set it up. Two separate parts of our server share the job, so that neither one alone can tell which chats are on which phone:

  • Your iPhone gives Frost an Apple push token. Frost keeps it only in memory. Before sending it anywhere, Frost seals it so that only our notifier can open it, and seals it afresh for each chat. The postbox stores these sealed handles next to each mailbox, but it can't open them or tell that two of them belong to the same phone.
  • When a message arrives, the postbox passes the sealed handles for that mailbox to the notifier, without saying which mailbox they came from. The notifier waits a random moment, opens them, and asks Apple to wake each phone. It has no database: push tokens exist in its memory only while a notification is being sent.
  • Every notification is identical and contains no content: no sender, no chat and no message. Apple learns only that Frost sent your phone a notification, and when. Your iPhone then replaces it with a disguise you choose in Frost's Settings (by default, a weather alert). Neither our server nor Apple ever learns your disguise. iOS still shows Frost's icon.
  • While Frost is open, new messages appear in the chat rather than as notifications.

If you turn notifications off, Frost still works: you see new messages when you open it. Some test builds don't have notifications set up; they never send a push token at all.

For now, the postbox and the notifier run on the same server, both run by us. Someone in control of that running server could match up the timing of the two. The notifier on its own can see which phones are woken at the same moment, so it could guess that they share a chat, though not which one.

Connecting: face to face only

You connect with someone only in person. One phone shows a code, and the other scans it with Frost's own scanner. There are no invite links: an invite is never sent through another app, a text message or the clipboard. The code holds a new mailbox's id and a secret. The postbox sees only the mailbox id; the secret, which unlocks the invite, never reaches it. Your name and public keys go into that mailbox sealed with the secret, so the postbox can't read them. An unused invite is deleted from the postbox after seven days, like any other message.

Scanning uses the camera, so iOS asks permission the first time. The camera is used only to read the code. Nothing is recorded or saved. You can compare safety numbers afterwards to make sure nobody is in the middle.

How messages disappear

  • When you open a message, it shows for the chat's reading time (between 10 seconds and 5 minutes, set when the chat is made), then it's gone from your phone.
  • A message you don't open within seven days expires on your phone unread.
  • Copies on the postbox are deleted seven days after they arrive, whatever happened to them.
  • When you open someone's message, Frost tells them, inside the encrypted chat, that you read it.

Leaving a group, deleting a chat, deleting the app

  • Leaving a group: your phone sends a sealed message saying you're leaving, then deletes the chat and its keys. The group's admin's phone then removes you, changes the group's keys and moves the group to a new mailbox, so you can't read anything sent after that. If you run the group, leaving closes it for everyone.
  • Deleting a chat destroys its messages and keys on your phone. Nobody else is told. The other person's app may still send messages to that chat's mailbox, and your phone may still get a disguised notification when they do, but those messages can't be opened.
  • Wipe everything in Settings destroys your keys, chats and waiting messages on your iPhone. It can't be undone.
  • Deleting Frost removes its files from your iPhone. iOS may keep Frost's keys in the Keychain after an app is deleted; if you install Frost again, it erases them when it first starts. To remove them right away, use Wipe everything before deleting the app.
  • On the postbox: nothing there says which mailboxes are yours, so there is no account for us to delete. Messages are deleted after seven days in every case. Once Frost is off your phone, Apple tells the notifier the next time it tries to wake you, and the postbox then deletes your sealed push handles.

Where the server runs

The postbox and the notifier run on a single rented server that we manage, reached at relay.methodical-ep.com. The postbox has no access to the internet; only the notifier can reach out, and only to Apple's push service. Builds we install directly from our own development computer for testing may use a test relay on that computer instead.

Nothing leaves, nothing is captured

  • Screenshots: screenshots, screen recordings and screen mirroring show a blank screen in place of Frost. This relies on a method iOS uses for password fields, and Settings shows whether it is working. Nothing can stop someone photographing the screen with another camera. If someone takes a screenshot while a message is open, the chat is told.
  • No sharing out: Frost's text fields offer only Paste. There is no Copy, Cut, Share, Look Up, Translate or Writing Tools, and opened messages can't be selected or copied. There is no way to send a message, photo or file out of Frost. Frost handles text only.
  • Keyboards: Frost allows only Apple's own keyboard, because other keyboards can record what you type. Autocorrect and spell checking are off in Frost.
  • Dictation: Frost can't turn off the keyboard's dictation button. If you dictate, iOS may send your speech to Apple to turn it into text, under Apple's privacy policy. Type instead if that matters to you.
  • The app switcher: Frost covers itself whenever it leaves the screen, before iOS takes the picture it shows in the app switcher.
  • No network leftovers: Frost talks to the postbox without saving any responses, cookies or connection records on your phone.

Face ID and passcode

Frost opens only after Face ID, Touch ID or your device passcode. This check is performed entirely by iOS. Frost is told only whether it succeeded — no biometric information is ever available to the app, to us, or to anyone else.

Passphrase-locked chats

You can lock a chat with a passphrase you agree face to face. Messages in it are sealed a second time with a key made from the passphrase. Frost never saves the passphrase or its key, and they never leave your phone. If everyone loses the passphrase, the chat can never be read again, and we can't help recover it.

Reporting someone

You can report a person from a chat's info. Reporting blocks them on your phone. Where reporting is set up, Frost then opens your Mail app with an email to us containing only the reason and what you type. Frost can't read anyone's messages, so it never attaches them. We receive only what you choose to send, and use it only to look into the report and answer you. In builds where reporting isn't set up yet, the person is blocked and nothing is sent.

Analytics, tracking, and advertising

Frost contains no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking of any kind. It uses no third-party services besides Apple's push service. Its encryption library (OpenMLS) is open source and runs on your phone. We do not build a profile of you and do not track you across apps or websites. There are no purchases, subscriptions or ads.

Children's privacy

Frost doesn't ask anyone for their age, phone number, email or real name, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children. Nothing on our server identifies who you are.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your chats live on your iPhone, and you control them: delete a chat, use Wipe everything, or delete the app. On our server, sealed messages are deleted after seven days, and sealed push handles are deleted once Apple reports your phone no longer has Frost. Because the postbox holds nothing that says who you are, we can't look up or hand over your data, and there is no account to close. There is no way to recover chats, keys or messages if you lose your iPhone.
